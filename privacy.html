Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 6, 2026

1. About Maya

Maya is an AI assistant maintained by its developer for that developer's own account and personal workflows. It is not offered as a public sign-up service or distributed commercially. This policy explains how its Gmail-connected workflows handle Google user data and information derived from that data.

2. Google Account Access and Email Processing

Maya uses an existing Desktop OAuth client and the gmail.modify permission. Access requires authorization from the Google account owner. The Gmail workflow is restricted to one designated account and an allowlist of senders. When enabled, it can read messages, send replies, and remove unread labels. Maya does not currently provide a Gmail message-deletion feature.

To identify eligible instructions, Maya searches the unread inbox and may retrieve message bodies and headers, including sender, recipient, subject, date, message and thread identifiers, labels, and authentication information. A candidate message may be read into memory before its sender or instruction is rejected. Access is therefore not limited to fetching only messages that are ultimately accepted as commands.

Authentication checks may retrieve the raw MIME message, which can include attachment data. The instruction handler rejects attachments and unsupported formats and checks for common forwarding and quotation markers. Rejected messages are not treated as accepted instructions or deliberately passed to the normal AI instruction pipeline. These checks do not guarantee perfect detection of quoted material or establish a person's identity. Email text is not an independent authorization for privileged actions.

3. Local Storage, Logs, and Backups

Accepted instructions and their results may be stored locally as task descriptions, outputs, review comments, activities, and improvement ideas, dialogues, or proposals. The email-processing ledger may retain message and thread identifiers, sender, subject, reply references, processing status, related task identifiers, result summaries, and timestamps. It also records delivery reservations and acknowledgements to reduce duplicate replies.

Operational logs and records can contain identifiers, status counts, error categories, task titles, tool events, and information derived from requests. Disabling AI tracing does not remove these local records. The application does not maintain a complete mailbox archive as its normal command store, but extracted instructions and derived content can persist.

Recovery backups are separate local copies. Credential backups contain OAuth credential material. Backups that include application data or reports may also retain Gmail-derived information. Removing an active record does not automatically erase its copies or backups.

4. External AI Processing and Data Sharing

Maya uses Google services for authorized Gmail operations. Workflows that require AI processing may send instruction text, selected workflow context, tool results, and generated artifacts to OpenAI's API. This information can include Gmail-derived content. Separately enabled research workflows may send approved source excerpts and the research request to an external AI service. Mail authentication can also require DNS lookups for public keys.

In the protected Gmail AI pathway, SDK tracing and sensitive trace contents are disabled and supported Responses requests use store=false. These settings are not a guarantee of zero retention or zero operational review by a service provider. Provider terms, account settings, and retention controls must be checked separately before enabling the relevant workflow.

Pattern-based checks block suspected secrets and certain personal data, but they do not guarantee complete anonymization or detection of every secret. Only information needed for the authorized assistant function should be sent. Maya is not intended to sell Google user data, use it for advertising, or initiate generalized AI-model training with it. Google-derived data must not be used by a provider for generalized-model training. The developer must verify compatible provider terms and controls before enabling such processing.

Use and transfer of Google API data must follow the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use rules. This policy does not authorize unrelated secondary uses of Google user data.

5. Retention, Deletion, and Revoking Access

There is no implemented app-wide automatic retention deadline for the email ledger, tasks, improvement records, artifacts, logs, or backups. Separate conversation-history cleanup and deletion helpers exist, but they do not establish a uniform Gmail-data retention period.

Maya does not currently provide a verified one-step process that erases all Gmail-derived records and backup copies. The account owner must separately identify the affected records, arrange an appropriate deletion procedure, and verify its scope and outcome. This policy does not promise immediate or automatic erasure. Recovery and audit copies require separate consideration.

The owner can stop using Maya and revoke its access in Google Account settings. Revocation does not automatically delete information already stored locally or held in backups, and does not by itself establish deletion by an external service provider.

6. Security

Maya checks the designated Gmail account and allowed sender information. Its email-admission checks use Google ARC signatures and associated DKIM/DMARC results rather than trusting an unverified header string alone. Unsupported or unverifiable input is rejected. These checks are security controls, not a guarantee that every message or instruction is trustworthy.

OAuth files and protected credential backups use owner-only file permissions and private directories. File-access controls check ownership, links, ACL evidence, and file identity, and restrict AI file tools from secret locations. The recovery process maintains TLS verification and validates new credentials before replacing an existing credential file.

Local permissions are not encryption. This review has not established that every local database, device, or backup is encrypted at rest. Application file-tool restrictions are also not an operating-system sandbox against arbitrary malicious code running as the same user. Google and external AI services have separate security and retention controls.

7. Owner Control and Changes

Maya is used only by its developer, who is also its sole account owner and administrator. Access, retention, and deletion decisions are handled directly by that owner on the device; Maya does not provide a public customer-support or automatic deletion service. A public support contact must be provided if the application is later offered to other users.

This policy will be reviewed when data access, processing, service providers, retention, or other relevant practices change. A revised policy is not a substitute for any additional authorization required for a new use of data.
